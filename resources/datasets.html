Recommended Free Data Sets for Data Analytics in Environment and International Affairs

Below are curated, high-quality, and free data sets suitable for your course. They are selected for relevance to environmental and international affairs, compatibility with R, and educational value for beginner to intermediate data analytics labs.

1. Environmental Data Sets

Open repo of ecological data for use in teaching

Global and Regional Environmental Data

  • WorldClim: High-resolution global climate data (temperature, precipitation, etc.), widely used for climate and ecological analysis[1][2].
  • USGS Earth Explorer: Satellite imagery, land cover, and environmental monitoring data for the United States and globally[1][2].
  • NOAA Climate Data Online (CDO): Historical weather and climate data, including temperature, precipitation, and wind, available globally[3][2].
  • Global Forest Watch: Deforestation, land use, and forest change data, including interactive maps and downloadable data[2][1].
  • OpenWeatherMap: Real-time and historical weather data accessible via API, suitable for time series and spatial analysis[1][2].

Specialized Environmental Data

  • LTER DataSampler R Package: Ready-to-use environmental data samples from the Long-Term Ecological Research (LTER) Network, ideal for R-based labs[4][5].
  • SAGE (Center for Sustainability and the Global Environment): Datasets on global ecosystems, croplands, air quality, and land use change[6].
  • Environmental Data Initiative (EDI): Repository of ecological and environmental datasets, including biodiversity, water quality, and ecosystem processes[5].
  • Global Solar Atlas & Global Wind Atlas: Renewable energy potential data for solar and wind resources worldwide[1][2].

2. International Affairs and Socio-Political Data Sets

International Relations and Political Data
  • World Bank Open Data: Comprehensive global development indicators, including environment, economy, health, and governance[7][8].
  • UNData: Aggregated data from United Nations agencies on topics such as environment, demographics, and development[7][9].
  • ACLED (Armed Conflict Location & Event Data Project): Geolocated data on political violence, protests, and conflict events globally[9][7].
  • Freedom House: Annual country-level data on political rights and civil liberties[9].
  • Worldwide Governance Indicators: Measures of governance quality for over 200 countries (voice, accountability, stability, rule of law, etc.)[9][7].
  • Correlates of War Project: Datasets on international conflict, alliances, and militarized disputes[10][7].

Socio-Economic and Health Data

  • WHO Health Inequality Data Repository: Disaggregated health and environmental indicators from global to local levels[11].
  • World Resources Institute: Data on climate, energy, food, forests, water, and urban issues at the intersection of environment and development[7].
  • Quality of Government Institute Data: Cross-national data on governance, corruption, and government effectiveness[9][7].

3. R-Compatible and Built-in Data Sets

  • R Built-in Datasets: R comes with a variety of datasets (e.g., airquality, CO2, iris, PlantGrowth, quakes, USArrests) useful for teaching data manipulation, visualization, and modeling[12][5].
  • Rdatasets Repository: Searchable collection of nearly 2,000 datasets from R packages, suitable for a range of analytics exercises[13].
  • Specialized R Packages:
    • climate: R package for accessing free meteorological and hydrological datasets globally[14].
    • peacesciencer: R package for peace and conflict studies datasets, including Correlates of War and UCDP data[10].
    • vdemdata: R package for democracy and governance indicators worldwide[10].

4. How to Use These Data Sets in R

  • Most global and institutional datasets are downloadable as CSV or Excel files, which can be imported into R using read.csv() or readxl::read_excel()[15][12].
  • Many R packages (e.g., climate, peacesciencer, LTERdatasampler) provide functions to download and load data directly into R, streamlining lab activities[14][4][10].
  • Built-in datasets can be accessed with the data() function in R, and their documentation is available with ?datasetname[12][13].

6. Additional Resources

  • Atlas.co Blog: Comprehensive guide to free environmental data sources, including global, regional, and geospatial repositories[1].
  • R-bloggers: Lists and reviews ecological and environmental data sources, including those built into R and available online[5].
  • Emory University Data Guide: Curated links to international relations and comparative politics datasets, many of which are R-compatible[8].

These data sets and resources provide a strong foundation for hands-on labs and projects in your course, ensuring students gain practical experience with real-world environmental and international affairs data[1][9][12].
